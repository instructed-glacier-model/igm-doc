    Publishing a study

    A study in the map viewer is a set of files archived on Zenodo, plus one entry in the viewer's catalogue. The website holds the page and the catalogue only; the viewer reads the data from the archive by HTTP range requests.

    Each study is the data of a paper and is named YEAR-FirstAuthor-JOURNAL, e.g. 2026-Doe-TC for a paper published in 2026 in The Cryosphere whose first author is J. Doe. Use the usual journal abbreviation (TC, GMD, JGlac, GRL, NatGeo, …). The viewer lists the studies by this name, most recent first.

    Steps

    1. Export the outputs

    igm_export_for_viewer converts the netCDF outputs of IGM (output.nc, dimensions time, y, x) into the files of a study. Describe the study in a file, here with two RGI regions:

    title: Glaciers of the Alps and Iceland to 2100
authors: [Jane Doe, John Roe]
year: 2026
journal: TC
doi: 10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXX
paper: https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-XX-XXXX-2026
licence: CC-BY-4.0
time_steps: every:5
layers: [thk, usurf, topg, smb, velsurf_mag]
layer_options:
  thk: {vmax: 800}
regions:
  alps:
    file: outputs/alps/output.nc
    crs: EPSG:32632
    label: Central Europe (RGI 11)
  iceland:
    file: outputs/iceland/output.nc
    crs: EPSG:32627
    label: Iceland (RGI 06)

    igm_export_for_viewer study.yaml --out viewer_export

    The study name is built from year, the family name of the first author and journal; give id to set it yourself. doi and paper are optional. time_steps is all, every:N or a list of times. layer_options overrides the default colour scale or labels of a layer (vmin, vmax, colormap, log, label, unit); valid_min and valid_max drop values outside a range, e.g. placeholder values in the netCDF. mask: ice keeps a layer only where there is ice (for a layer that does not change in time, where there is ice at any time step), mask: domain everywhere. crs is needed when the netCDF has no pyproj_srs attribute. Region names are short lowercase words; they appear in the file names and in the links to the viewer.

    file may also be a list of files or a pattern (outputs/C*.nc): runs made on tiles of one aligned grid, with the same times, are merged into one map. Merging keeps the number of files small, as a Zenodo record holds at most 100. time_offset is added to the times of the netCDF, e.g. time_offset: 2020 for runs whose time starts at 0 in 2020. igm_version records the version of IGM used for the runs when it is not the installed one.

    The 3D view of the viewer uses usurf for the ice surface and topg elsewhere: keep both layers to offer it.

    Scenarios

    When the study compares several runs of the same regions (climate scenarios, sensitivity experiments, …), list them under scenarios: instead of regions:. Each scenario has a label and its own regions, which must be the same in every scenario and on the same grid:

    scenarios:
  ssp126:
    label: SSP1-2.6
    regions:
      alps: {file: outputs/alps_ssp126/output.nc}
      iceland: {file: outputs/iceland_ssp126/output.nc}
  ssp585:
    label: SSP5-8.5
    regions:
      alps: {file: outputs/alps_ssp585/output.nc}
      iceland: {file: outputs/iceland_ssp585/output.nc}

    Keys given at the top of the study file (crs, time_offset, …) apply to every scenario, and can be set again in a scenario or a region. The viewer then shows one button per scenario, keeps the place, layer, year and colour scale when switching, and draws one curve per scenario in the time series of a cell. The colour scale of a layer is the same for all scenarios. Scenario names are short lowercase words.

    Studies made of GeoTIFF tiles

    Global studies are often delivered as one GeoTIFF per glacier and layer, each in its own projection. Give them under tiles: instead of regions::

    tiles:
  layers:
    thk: "data/Thk/RGI-*/**/*_thk.tif"
    topg: "data/Topg/RGI-*/**/*_topg.tif"
  group: "RGI-(\\d\\d)"
  name: "rgi{group}"
  labels: {"11": "Central Europe", "13": "Central Asia"}
  resolution: 100

    Files of the same glacier share their name up to the last _. Tiles are grouped by the part of their path matched by group; within a group, nearby glaciers are gathered into regions (gap_km, default 150), each on its own equal-area grid at resolution metres and below max_cells cells (default 2e8). Regions are named after name, with -1, -2, … when a group is split. Such studies have no time axis.

    A study with a single run can be exported without a study file:

    igm_export_for_viewer outputs/alps/output.nc --id 2026-Doe-TC --region alps --crs EPSG:32632 \
    --title "Glaciers of the Alps to 2100" --out viewer_export

    Layers missing from a netCDF are skipped; speeds are computed from their components when needed. The tool prints the size of each layer and writes, in the output folder, the two files to upload (igm_viewer.json and data.bin) and studies_entry.json, the entry for the catalogue.

    2. Check it locally

    igm_export_for_viewer --serve viewer_export

    opens a local server and prints a link to the viewer showing the exported study, before anything is uploaded.

    3. Upload to Zenodo

    Create a new Zenodo record titled like the study, upload the two files igm_viewer.json and data.bin as they are, and publish it:

    Zenodo record 1234567   2026-Doe-TC   doi:10.5281/zenodo.1234567
├── igm_viewer.json     description of the study, its layers and where their data are in data.bin
└── data.bin            the data: Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs placed one after the other

    A study of any size fits in these two files (a very large study gets data_1.bin, data_2.bin, … of 20 GB each), so the record stays far below the Zenodo limits of 100 files and 50 GB. Note the record number.

    4. Add the study to the catalogue

    The catalogue is the file docs/viewer/app/studies.json of the documentation repository. To add a study, open a pull request:

    1. On GitHub, open docs/viewer/app/studies.json and click the pencil (Edit this file). GitHub makes a fork of the repository for you.

    2. Paste the content of studies_entry.json into the list, replace <record> by the record number, and add the DOI of the data:

      {"studies": [
  {"id": "2026-Doe-TC", "title": "Glaciers of the Alps and Iceland to 2100",
   "base_url": "https://zenodo.org/api/records/1234567/files", "url_suffix": "/content",
   "doi": "10.5281/zenodo.1234567"}
]}

      Separate entries by commas; their order does not matter.

    3. Click Commit changes, then Propose changes, and Create pull request. Give the study name as title and the paper in the description.

    The maintainers check that the study opens in the viewer and merge the pull request. The study appears on the website at the next deployment of the documentation, and can then be linked directly, e.g. https://igm-model.org/latest/viewer/#study=2026-Doe-TC&region=iceland&layer=thk&t=2050.

    Format

    The data of a study are Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs in the CRS of the model grid, with 256 × 256 blocks and overviews, one per region, scenario, layer and chunk of time steps. The time steps of a chunk are stored together in each pixel (INTERLEAVE=PIXEL, band k described as TIME=<year>): one request returns a block for every step of the chunk, which keeps animations and time series fast and within the request limits of Zenodo. A chunk holds the whole series up to 40 steps, and 25 steps beyond (time_chunk in the study file or --time-chunk to change it). Values are stored as int16, stored = round(value × scale), nodata -32768; layers that do not change in time have a single band. Ice fields are masked where thk is below 1 m (ice_threshold in the study file).

    data.bin holds these files one after the other, and igm_viewer.json (schema igm_viewer/2) gives the scenarios, the regions with their grid, the layers with their scale, unit, label and default colour scale, the time axis, and for each chunk its file name and position in data.bin. With more than one region, a 1 to a few km overview of the last time step makes a world overview region, clickable to open each region (overview: true or false in the study file to force it).

    To get the GeoTIFFs as plain files, for QGIS, GDAL or rasterio:

    igm_export_for_viewer --unpack viewer_export

    writes them into viewer_export/files. Export with --no-container to get the plain files directly, e.g. to host a study elsewhere than on Zenodo; the server must then answer HTTP range requests and send Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *.

    Size

    Glacier-masked, one time step of one layer takes about 0.1 to 0.5 MB per million grid cells. For example, the European Alps at 100 m (17 million cells) with 3 scenarios, 5 layers and 7 time steps take 36 MB. A screen reads a few blocks of the chunk holding the current step; moving in time, playing and time series then need few or no more requests. Use time_steps: every:N to thin long runs.
