    Module thk

    Brief summary

    The thk module solves the mass conservation equation \(\partial_t h + \nabla\cdot(h\,\bar{\mathbf{u}}) = \mathrm{smb} + \mathrm{bmb}\) to update the ice thickness thk from the depth-averaged velocity of the iceflow module and the mass balances of the smb (and, for marine glaciers, bmb) modules, and recomputes the surfaces usurf and lsurf with flotation. The transport is solved by an explicit slope-limited finite-volume scheme (Jouvet et al., 2021)1 (the default) or by the alternative schemes below; for marine- and lake-terminated glaciers it can track a calving front at sub-grid scale. The parameters of the module are described here.

    State variables

    Reads: thk, topg, water_level, ubar, vbar, dt

    Writes: thk, lsurf, usurf, divflux, Href, psi, calved_thk, calving_unapplied_thk, ice_area_fraction

    Transport schemes

    The scheme is selected by scheme:

    • explicit (default) — explicit upwind finite volumes on the 2D grid. Edge fluxes are computed from the depth-averaged velocity and the upwind thickness, with a piecewise-linear reconstruction of the thickness at the cell edges by the Superbee slope limiter (Roe, 1986)2 (slope_type: superbee, minmod or godunov), which keeps the scheme monotone (total variation diminishing). The scheme is mass-conservative and fully parallel, but subject to a CFL condition: the time step of the time module is bounded by processes.time.cfl, the maximum number of cells crossed in one step. With divflux_smooth_sigma > 0 the staggered fluxes are Gaussian-filtered before the divergence (conservative flux smoothing).
    • ffsl — conservative, monotone flux-form semi-Lagrangian transport with swept fluxes, which allows arbitrary CFL numbers; internal substeps are controlled by the velocity deformation (ffsl.max_deformation, ffsl.max_substeps, ffsl.limit_policy).
    • implicit — the diffusive first-order upwind theta method in 2D (implicit.theta: 1 backward Euler, 0.5 Crank–Nicolson), solved by a matrix-free, Jacobi-preconditioned BiCGSTAB (implicit.solver).
    • implicit_x — independent x-flowlines solved directly with one batched tridiagonal solve, without y transport.

    The schemes ffsl, implicit and implicit_x are opt-in; adi is an experimental alternating-direction method.

    Domain and boundaries

    boundary.{left,right,top,bottom} set the transport boundary of each side of the grid: zero (alias open, zero exterior ice, outflow permitted), symmetric (aliases closed, no_flux, reflective), dirichlet (alias inflow, the exterior thickness held at the initial thickness of the edge cells, so that ice enters with the edge velocity) or periodic (paired sides). The explicit scheme and the calving fronts support zero, symmetric and dirichlet; FFSL and implicit support zero, symmetric and periodic; implicit_x supports zero and symmetric in x.

    domain.constraints restricts the active cells (e.g. grounded, initial_ice, interior, state_mask); internal faces between active and inactive cells carry zero flux.

    With remove_rigid_body_modes: true, floating ice without a mechanical anchor to grounded ice (icebergs, i.e. ice not connected through full cells to a cell with a grounded corner) is removed after every update and once at initialization, because its velocity is not determined by the force balance. Grounded ice is never removed; the removed ice is counted in calved_thk.

    Flotation

    The lower surface is \(\mathrm{lsurf} = \max(\mathrm{topg},\ \mathrm{water\_level} - h\,\rho_\mathrm{i}/\rho_\mathrm{w})\) with the density ratio ratio_density, and \(\mathrm{usurf} = \mathrm{lsurf} + h\); ice is grounded where its base rests on the bed. The water level water_level (sea or lake level) is read from the input; without an ocean it takes a "no ocean" default and all ice is grounded.

    Calving front

    For marine and lake termini, front.method tracks the position of the calving front within the grid cells, so that the front stays sharp instead of spreading over the ocean as a thin diffusive tongue:

    • none (default) — the front is left to the transport scheme.
    • sub_grid — the front is tracked from the volume of the partial cells, after Albrecht et al. (2011) as in PISM: ice flowing into an ice-free cell next to the front accumulates in a reservoir Href until the cell is full; the cell then joins the ice at the full thickness and the residual volume is redistributed to its ice-free neighbours (sub_grid.residual: redistribute, up to sub_grid.max_iterations passes) or discarded (discard, counted in calved_thk).
    • level_set — the front is tracked by a level-set function psi advected at \(\mathbf{u} - (c + m_\mathrm{cf})\,\mathbf{n}\) (after Bondzio et al., 2016), with the ice following it exactly (mass-consistent); the function is re-distanced every level_set.reinit_freq steps, and the ice velocity is extended level_set.band cells beyond the ice.

    In both methods thk is the physical thickness seen by the ice flow: the stress-balance front is sharp on the boundary of full cells, while the partial cells keep their volume in Href and their fill fraction in ice_area_fraction. The thickness a filling cell takes is given by front.threshold: flux (from the flux into it, i.e. thickness continuity) or mean (the mean of its neighbours, as in PISM). front.first_order uses first-order upwind fluxes out of the cells next to the front (Albrecht et al., 2011, Eq. 2).

    The calving rate \(c\) and the frontal melt rate \(m_\mathrm{cf}\) come from the calving_rate module. Two rules prescribe the front position directly: front.min_thickness calves floating front cells thinner than this (the thickness rule of Albrecht et al., 2011, and PISM's thickness_calving), and front.fixed keeps the front from advancing beyond its initial position. The calved volume is accumulated in calved_thk. Both methods require scheme: explicit and no active-domain constraints.

    Module ordering

    thk runs after iceflow (and after smb, bmb and calving_rate when used) and before time advances; the usual order is [time, smb, iceflow, thk], or for a marine glacier [time, ocean, iceflow, calving_rate, bmb, thk].

    Example usage

    A marine glacier with a sub-grid front, minimum front thickness and icebergs removed:

    # @package _global_

processes:
  thk:
    front:
      method: sub_grid
      min_thickness: 50.0      # m
    remove_rigid_body_modes: true
  calving_rate:
    law: water_depth
    water_depth:
      k: 2.0                   # 1/yr

    Description of the parameters:

    Name Description Default value Units
    scheme Thickness transport backend: explicit is the production-default slope-limited forward-Euler method; ffsl is conservative monotone flux-form semi-Lagrangian transport with arbitrary-CFL swept fluxes; implicit is the diffusive 2-D first-order-upwind theta method solved by matrix-free, Jacobi-preconditioned BiCGSTAB; implicit_x solves independent x-flowlines directly with one batched tridiagonal solve and no y transport; adi is the experimental second-order Peaceman-Rachford alternating-direction method. A front backend may compose with or explicitly replace transport. explicit —
    slope_type Type of slope limiter for the explicit ice-thickness equation (godunov, minmod, or superbee). The FFSL scheme requires its monotone Superbee reconstruction. superbee —
    divflux_smooth_sigma If > 0, Gaussian-filter the staggered fluxes Qx/Qy (std in grid cells) before taking the divergence in the explicit thickness transport. The filtered flux remains conservative; 0 disables filtering. 0.0 —
    ratio_density Density of ice divided by density of water, used by the flotation term. If iceflow densities are configured, initialization verifies that their ratio is consistent with this value. 0.91 —
    remove_rigid_body_modes If true, ice without a mechanical anchor to grounded ice is removed after every thickness update (and once at initialization). Floating ice must belong to full Q1 cells connected through cell edges to a full cell with a grounded corner; otherwise it can translate or rotate freely, so its velocity is not determined by the force balance and exact solves return arbitrary speeds that throttle the CFL step. Grounded ice is never removed. With a calving front, nodes edge-adjacent to anchored ice (just filled, waiting for their neighbours to form full cells) are kept, the partial-cell reservoir Href is kept only next to the remaining ice, and the removed ice is added to calved_thk. False —
    domain
    Name Description Default value Units
    domain.constraints Active-cell constraints intersected before thickness transport. Each entry has a method: grounded recomputes flotation from live geometry; initial_ice retains cells above its optional min_thickness at initialization; interior retains the four-neighbour interior of a named state field; state_mask uses a named state field directly. An empty list evolves the full grid. Internal active/inactive faces carry zero flux, and inactive thickness is held fixed. [] —
    boundary
    Name Description Default value Units
    boundary.left Transport boundary at the left x side: zero (alias open) assumes zero exterior ice and permits outflow; symmetric (aliases closed, no_flux, reflective) imposes zero normal mass flux; dirichlet (alias inflow) holds the exterior thickness at the initial thickness of the edge cells, so ice enters with the edge velocity; periodic connects to the right side and requires boundary.right=periodic. Explicit and the calving fronts support zero, symmetric and dirichlet; implicit_x supports zero and symmetric in x; FFSL and implicit support zero, symmetric and periodic. zero —
    boundary.right Transport boundary at the right x side, with the same choices as boundary.left. This may differ from the left side except that periodic boundaries must be paired. zero —
    boundary.top Transport boundary at the first row of the y dimension, with the same zero/open, symmetric/no-flux, dirichlet/inflow and paired-periodic choices. zero —
    boundary.bottom Transport boundary at the last row of the y dimension. This may differ from the top side except that periodic boundaries must be paired. zero —
    implicit
    Name Description Default value Units
    implicit.theta Theta-method weight in [0.5, 1.0] for implicit and implicit_x. The default 1.0 is backward Euler; 0.5 is Crank-Nicolson. 1.0 —
    implicit.solver.tol Requested relative residual tolerance for the implicit BiCGSTAB solve. The effective tolerance includes a small roundoff floor proportional to machine epsilon times the largest operator diagonal; use float64 when a tighter high-CFL solve is required. 1e-06 —
    implicit.solver.max_iter Maximum number of implicit BiCGSTAB iterations per thickness step. 1000 —
    implicit.solver.max_restarts Maximum low-memory BiCGSTAB residual-replacement restarts after numerical breakdown or recursive-residual drift. 8 —
    implicit.solver.failure_policy Action when the implicit solver does not converge: stop retains the old thickness, sets state.thk_step_accepted=false, and stops the run without a host synchronization; accept uses the best available iterate. stop —
    ffsl
    Name Description Default value Units
    ffsl.max_deformation Maximum velocity-deformation number per internal FFSL step. Internal substeps are based on dt times an upper bound for the velocity-gradient norm, not on uniform-translation CFL; the default 0.5 preserves ordered departure faces and monotonicity. 0.5 —
    ffsl.max_substeps Maximum number of deformation-controlled FFSL substeps per outer thickness step. If this safety limit is reached, state.ffsl_substep_limit_reached is true and state.ffsl_required_substeps reports the uncapped request. 128 —
    ffsl.limit_policy Action when FFSL requires more than max_substeps: stop retains the old thickness, sets state.thk_step_accepted=false, and stops the run without a host synchronization; accept applies the capped step. stop —
    front
    Name Description Default value Units
    front.method Calving front of marine (and lake) termini: none leaves the front to the thickness transport (ice advected into an ice-free cell spreads over it); sub_grid tracks the front at sub-grid scale from the volume of the partial cells (Albrecht et al., 2011, as in PISM); level_set tracks it with a level-set function moving at u - (c + m_cf) n (after Bondzio et al., 2016), with the ice following it exactly. In both, thk is the physical thickness seen by the ice flow: a front cell joins the ice at the full thickness once its cell is full, so the stress-balance front is sharp on the boundary of full Q1 cells, while the partial cells keep their volume in Href. The calving rate c and the frontal melt m_cf come from the calving_rate process. Both methods require scheme: explicit and no active-domain constraints. none —
    front.first_order Use first-order upwind fluxes out of the cells next to the front, so that the flux into a partial cell is the ice-side flux u H (Albrecht et al., 2011, Eq. 2); the bulk keeps thk.slope_type. True —
    front.min_thickness Calve floating front cells thinner than this (the thickness rule of Albrecht et al., 2011, and PISM's thickness_calving); 0 disables it. 0.0 m
    front.fixed The front cannot advance beyond its initial position: ice flowing past it is calved (the front may still retreat). False —
    front.threshold Thickness H_r a partial cell takes when it fills: flux gives it the thickness continuity gives to the ice entering it, the mean over its ice neighbours of H_n |u_n| / |u_p| with |u_p| the speed extrapolated linearly to the cell (exact for a steady spreading shelf, where u H is constant; the mean where the speed is uniform); mean is the mean of the neighbours (PISM), which on a front thinning downstream leaves a wall one cell thick that travels with the front (Albrecht et al., 2011, Sect. 3), a first-order error. Where the bed is above the neighbours' mean ice base, the cell is filled to their mean surface in both cases. flux —
    front.sub_grid.max_iterations Passes redistributing the residual volume of partial cells that fill (PISM geometry.part_grid.max_iterations); what is left is kept in place. 10 —
    front.sub_grid.residual Residual volume of a filled partial cell: redistribute it equally among its ice-free ocean neighbours (PISM), or discard it (Albrecht et al., 2011, variant 1), counted in calved_thk. redistribute —
    front.level_set.reinit_freq Re-distance the level-set function psi every N steps (0: never). The partial cells are frozen (the full and empty cells re-clamped after), so no fill fraction or mass changes; every step (the default) keeps the upwind front speed exact. 1 steps
    front.level_set.reinit_iter Pseudo-time iterations of each re-distancing (Sussman et al., 1994). 5 —
    front.level_set.band Cells beyond the ice where the ice velocity is extended for the level-set advection. 3 cells
